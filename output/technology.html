Artificial Intelligence (AI) is transforming the automotive industry at an unprecedented pace. From manufacturing and vehicle design to maintenance and customer service, AI is helping businesses improve efficiency, reduce costs, and deliver better experiences. As connected vehicles and electric mobility continue to gain momentum, AI is becoming a key driver of innovation across the automotive value chain. One area benefiting significantly from this technological shift is the India Automotive Aftermarket Market, where intelligent technologies are reshaping vehicle servicing, spare parts management, and repair operations.

AI is Reshaping Automotive Manufacturing

Automotive manufacturers are increasingly relying on AI to streamline production and improve quality. Modern factories generate massive amounts of operational data, and AI helps analyze this information to identify bottlenecks, optimize workflows, and reduce waste.

Computer vision systems inspect vehicle components with greater precision than manual methods, while AI-powered robots perform repetitive assembly tasks with speed and consistency. Predictive analytics also allows manufacturers to monitor equipment performance and schedule maintenance before machinery fails, reducing downtime and increasing productivity.

These innovations enable manufacturers to produce safer, more reliable vehicles while lowering operating costs.

Predictive Maintenance Is Changing Vehicle Servicing

One of AI’s most valuable applications in the automotive industry is predictive maintenance. Instead of relying on fixed service schedules, AI continuously analyzes data collected from sensors installed throughout the vehicle.

This technology can monitor:

  • Engine performance
  • Brake condition
  • Battery health
  • Tire pressure
  • Cooling systems
  • Transmission efficiency

When abnormal patterns are detected, the system alerts vehicle owners or service providers before a major failure occurs. Early intervention reduces repair costs, prevents unexpected breakdowns, and extends the lifespan of critical components.

For commercial fleet operators, predictive maintenance also minimizes downtime and improves operational efficiency.

Smarter Diagnostics Improve Repair Accuracy

Vehicle diagnostics have evolved significantly with the introduction of AI. Traditional troubleshooting often depends on manual inspections and technician experience, but AI-powered diagnostic tools can analyze thousands of data points within seconds.

These systems identify faults more accurately and recommend appropriate repair procedures. They can detect problems involving electrical systems, engines, transmissions, batteries, and onboard sensors before symptoms become severe.

As a result, workshops can complete repairs faster while improving service quality and customer satisfaction.

AI Is Accelerating Electric Vehicle Growth

Electric vehicles require advanced monitoring systems to ensure optimal performance and safety. AI plays a critical role in managing battery performance, thermal conditions, charging cycles, and energy consumption.

Modern Battery Management Systems (BMS) use machine learning algorithms to continuously evaluate battery health and optimize charging behaviour. This improves driving range, extends battery lifespan, and reduces the likelihood of unexpected failures.

AI also supports research into new battery materials, allowing manufacturers to develop higher-capacity batteries with faster charging capabilities and improved durability.

As electric mobility expands, service centres are adapting by investing in specialized diagnostic equipment and technician training to support next-generation vehicles.

AI Is Making Supply Chains More Efficient

Managing automotive components and spare parts is becoming increasingly complex as vehicle technologies evolve. AI helps manufacturers, distributors, and service providers forecast demand more accurately by analyzing historical sales, regional trends, seasonal demand, and vehicle usage patterns.

Smarter inventory management offers several benefits:

  • Reduced stock shortages
  • Lower inventory costs
  • Faster order fulfillment
  • Improved warehouse efficiency
  • Better supply chain visibility

These improvements ensure that essential parts are available when needed while minimizing excess inventory.

Transforming Customer Experience

Customer expectations are changing rapidly, and AI is helping automotive businesses provide more personalized and convenient services.

Many companies now use AI-powered platforms to offer:

  • Online service scheduling
  • Digital maintenance records
  • Automated service reminders
  • Virtual customer support
  • Personalized maintenance recommendations
  • Real-time repair updates

These digital services reduce waiting times, improve communication, and create a smoother ownership experience.

AI Is Supporting Smarter Fleet Management

Fleet operators manage hundreds or even thousands of vehicles, making efficient maintenance essential for profitability. AI enables fleet managers to monitor vehicle performance continuously and identify opportunities to improve efficiency.

AI-powered fleet management systems analyze:

  • Fuel consumption
  • Driver behavior
  • Vehicle health
  • Route efficiency
  • Maintenance requirements

These insights help businesses reduce operating expenses, improve safety, and maximize vehicle availability.

Sustainability Through Artificial Intelligence

Sustainability has become a major focus across the automotive industry. AI contributes by helping manufacturers optimize production processes, reduce material waste, and improve energy efficiency.

AI also supports recycling initiatives by identifying reusable materials from vehicle components and improving the sorting of recyclable parts. In addition, intelligent manufacturing systems minimize energy consumption while maintaining consistent product quality.

As companies work toward lower carbon emissions, AI will continue to play an important role in building a more sustainable automotive ecosystem.

Challenges to AI Adoption

Although AI offers significant benefits, its implementation is not without challenges.

High Initial Investment

Advanced AI software, connected equipment, and automation technologies require substantial investment, particularly for smaller businesses.

Workforce Development

The industry needs skilled professionals who understand artificial intelligence, data analytics, robotics, and modern vehicle technologies. Continuous training is essential as vehicles become increasingly software-driven.

Cybersecurity

Connected vehicles and digital manufacturing systems generate vast amounts of data. Protecting this information from cyber threats is becoming a top priority for automotive companies.

Technology Integration

Many organizations still operate legacy systems that are difficult to integrate with modern AI platforms. Successful digital transformation requires careful planning and long-term investment.

The Future of AI in the Automotive Industry

Artificial Intelligence will continue to shape the future of mobility over the coming decade. Emerging technologies such as digital twins, edge computing, autonomous systems, and generative AI will further improve manufacturing efficiency, vehicle safety, and maintenance capabilities.

Connected vehicles will generate even larger volumes of real-time data, enabling more accurate diagnostics and predictive maintenance. Workshops will increasingly rely on AI-powered tools to identify faults, recommend repairs, and manage inventory more efficiently.

At the same time, electric vehicles will drive demand for specialized servicing, battery monitoring, and software-based maintenance solutions. Companies that embrace AI early will be better positioned to adapt to evolving customer expectations and technological advancements.

Conclusion

Artificial Intelligence is no longer an emerging technology—it has become a fundamental part of the automotive industry’s transformation. From intelligent manufacturing and predictive maintenance to advanced diagnostics and smarter supply chains, AI is improving efficiency across every stage of the vehicle lifecycle.

As the industry continues its transition toward connected and electric mobility, businesses that invest in AI-driven innovation will gain a competitive advantage through better operational performance, improved customer experiences, and more sustainable practices. The combination of intelligent technologies, digital infrastructure, and data-driven decision-making will define the next generation of automotive services, creating a more efficient, reliable, and future-ready mobility ecosystem.

Lightweight Strength for a Sustainable Future

Composite materials are reshaping the automotive industry by offering a unique combination of strength, durability, and lightweight properties. As car manufacturers face mounting pressures to improve fuel efficiency, reduce emissions, enhance vehicle safety, and meet sustainability goals, composites have emerged as game-changers in vehicle design and manufacturing. This blog explores how advanced composite materials are revolutionizing automotive engineering and driving innovation across multiple vehicle segments.

Why Composite Materials Matter in Automotive Manufacturing

The quest for lighter vehicles that do not compromise strength and safety is at the heart of modern automotive innovation. Composite materials, such as carbon fiber-reinforced polymers (CFRP) and glass fiber composites, deliver exceptional strength-to-weight ratios. Unlike traditional metals, composites provide rigidity and durability while significantly reducing vehicle mass. This results in enhanced fuel economy, lower greenhouse gas emissions, and improved driving performance.

Moreover, composites are inherently corrosion-resistant, unlike steel and aluminum, ensuring longer-lasting automotive components. Their design flexibility allows complex shapes and integrated functions to be molded efficiently, opening new horizons for innovative vehicle aesthetics and ergonomics. From sleek exterior panels to structural chassis components, composites provide automakers with the freedom to rethink traditional design constraints. According to DataM Intelligence latest report automotive composite materials market is expected to reach US$ 71.01 billion by 2031, growing with a CAGR of 10.75% during the forecast period 2024-2031.

Key Composite Types Driving Automotive Change

Significant types of composite materials are making waves in automotive engineering:

Carbon Fiber Composites: Prized for their extremely high strength and lightweight properties, carbon fiber composites are prominent in luxury, sports, and electric vehicles. They contribute to performance by reducing weight and improving acceleration, handling, and braking while maintaining crash safety.

Glass Fiber Composites: Widely used due to cost-effectiveness and good mechanical properties, glass fiber-reinforced plastics are often found in mid-range vehicles for body panels, bumpers, and structural components, balancing performance and affordability.

Thermoplastic Composites: Exhibiting excellent crash energy absorption, thermoplastics offer better recyclability and manufacturing efficiency. They eliminate painting steps thanks to surface film technology and generate zero solvent emissions during production, aligning with environmental mandates.

Natural Fiber Composites: Emerging as sustainable alternatives, composites using fibers like flax and hemp combined with biodegradable matrices reduce environmental impact without sacrificing mechanical strength. These bio-composites are typically applied in interiors, door panels, and trunk liners.

Transforming Automotive Components and Systems

 From structural to cosmetic parts, composite materials are integrated into diverse automotive applications:

Chassis and Body Panels: Composites replace heavier metal parts, offering reduced vehicle weight without compromising rigidity. Honeycomb-structured composites provide superior energy absorption during collisions, elevating passenger safety and meeting crash regulations.

Brake Systems: Composite brake pads and rotors outperform traditional metals in wear resistance and thermal stability. These components improve brake longevity and noise reduction, contributing to enhanced driving comfort.

Powertrain Components: Composite materials, particularly ceramic matrix composites (CMCs), enable high-temperature resistance crucial for engine turbochargers, exhaust manifolds, and transmission housings. Their thermal stability optimizes heat management, boosting powertrain efficiency.

Interiors: Composite materials allow innovative, lightweight designs for seats, dashboards, and door trims. The ability to mold intricate shapes and integrate features—like built-in electronics and acoustic insulation—enhances vehicle comfort and functionality.

Regional Adoption and Industry Trends

In Europe and North America, composites are heavily adopted by luxury and high-performance vehicle manufacturers due to their premium properties. Emerging markets and mainstream automakers increasingly incorporate composites to comply with tightening fuel-efficiency standards and reduce carbon footprints.

Research and development centers across Germany, France, and the U.K. focus on pushing composite fabrication technologies for mass production scalability. Moreover, manufacturers invest in sustainable composites and circular economy approaches, including recycling composite waste and using natural fibers.

Overcoming Challenges in Composite Integration

While composites offer many advantages, initial cost concerns and production complexities remain hurdles for broad adoption, especially in high-volume models. The automotive industry traditionally invests heavily in metal stamping facilities, causing a cautious approach to switching to composite manufacturing.

However, advancements in automation, including rapid tape laying and additive manufacturing, are reducing production times and costs. Combining simulation tools for stress analysis and manufacturing optimization enables faster design-to-production cycles. These developments are gradually overcoming traditional barriers, making composites more viable for mainstream vehicle production.

The Future of Composites in Automobiles

Collaborative innovation among material scientists, automotive engineers, and manufacturers will continue unlocking the full potential of composites. By fine-tuning fiber orientations, matrix systems, and hybrid composite designs, automakers can tailor performance characteristics for specific platforms, whether for electric vehicles, autonomous cars, or commercial trucks.

The growing demand for lightweight, durable, and environmentally friendly vehicles makes composites a cornerstone of future automotive design. As legislation pushes for carbon-neutral manufacturing and operability, composites will play a pivotal role in ensuring vehicles meet these exigent standards while delivering enhanced safety and driving experience.

Conclusion

 

Composite materials are at the forefront of transforming the automotive industry. Their unmatched strength-to-weight ratio, corrosion resistance, and design flexibility are enabling manufacturers to build safer, lighter, and more efficient vehicles. Technological advances in composite fabrication and increased sustainability focus will broaden their adoption across all vehicle segments. As composites continue to redefine what is possible in automotive engineering, they promise a future where innovation meets environmental responsibility and superior performance.

The automotive sector’s embrace of composite materials marks a decisive move towards smarter, greener, and more stylish vehicles powering tomorrow’s roads.

The automotive industry stands at the forefront of a technological revolution, driven by the integration of automation and robotics. This transformation is not merely a trend but a fundamental shift that enhances efficiency, precision, and productivity across the manufacturing process. From assembly lines to quality control, automation and robotics are redefining the landscape of automotive manufacturing, enabling unprecedented levels of innovation and operational excellence.

Evolution of Automation in Automotive Manufacturing

Automation in the automotive industry has evolved significantly over the decades. Early implementations were rudimentary, focusing on repetitive tasks and basic assembly processes. However, advancements in technology have led to sophisticated systems capable of performing complex operations with high precision. Today’s automotive factories are equipped with state-of-the-art robotic arms, automated guided vehicles (AGVs), and advanced software systems that streamline production, reduce human error, and ensure consistent quality.

Revolutionizing Manufacturing Processes

The adoption of automation and robotics in the automotive sector is revolutionizing manufacturing processes. Advanced robotics can handle complex tasks such as welding, painting, and assembly with greater efficiency and accuracy than human workers. This revolution is characterized by the use of collaborative robots (cobots) that work alongside human operators, enhancing productivity while maintaining safety standards.

Enhancing Efficiency and Productivity

Automation and robotics significantly enhance efficiency and productivity in automotive manufacturing. Robots can work continuously without fatigue, leading to higher output and faster production cycles. Automated systems streamline the entire production process, from material handling to final assembly, reducing cycle times and increasing throughput. For instance, robotic welding systems can perform thousands of welds per day with precision that is difficult to achieve manually.

Improving Quality and Consistency

One of the primary advantages of automation and robotics is the improvement in quality and consistency. Robots are capable of executing tasks with a high degree of accuracy, reducing variability and ensuring uniformity in manufacturing. This precision translates to improved product quality and reduced waste, as robots can consistently perform intricate tasks such as painting and assembly with minimal errors.

Reducing Costs and Increasing Sustainability

Automation and robotics contribute to cost reduction and sustainability in automotive manufacturing. By optimizing processes and minimizing waste, these technologies help manufacturers reduce production costs. Additionally, automated systems are energy-efficient and can be programmed to use resources optimally, reducing the environmental footprint of manufacturing operations. The integration of automation also reduces the reliance on manual labor, leading to significant cost savings in the long run.

Addressing Labor Shortages and Skill Gaps

The automotive industry faces labor shortages and skill gaps, which automation and robotics can help address. By automating repetitive and physically demanding tasks, manufacturers can allocate human workers to more strategic and value-added roles. This shift not only improves workforce utilization but also reduces the impact of labor shortages on production. Moreover, training programs focused on robotics and automation skills can help bridge the skill gap and prepare the workforce for the future.

Enabling Customization and Innovation

Automation and robotics enable greater customization and innovation in automotive manufacturing. Modern robotic systems are highly adaptable, allowing manufacturers to reconfigure production lines quickly in response to changing market demands. This flexibility is crucial for accommodating different vehicle models and customizations without significant downtime or retooling costs. Additionally, automation fosters innovation by enabling manufacturers to experiment with new designs and processes, driving continuous improvement and competitiveness.

Key Areas of Impact

  1. Assembly Lines: Automated assembly lines are the backbone of modern automotive manufacturing. Robots perform tasks such as welding, bolting, and material handling with unmatched speed and accuracy. This automation reduces the time required to assemble a vehicle, improving overall production throughput.
  2. Material Handling: Automated guided vehicles (AGVs) and autonomous mobile robots (AMRs) play a vital role in material handling within factories. These robots transport components and materials between workstations, ensuring a seamless flow of resources and minimizing delays.
  3. Quality Control: Advanced robotic systems equipped with sensors and machine vision are employed for quality control and inspection. These systems can detect defects at a microscopic level, ensuring that only components meeting stringent quality standards proceed to the next stage of production.
  4. Painting and Coating: Robotic arms are extensively used for painting and coating applications. They provide a consistent finish, reduce paint wastage, and operate in hazardous environments without health risks to human workers.

Learning from the Best

  1. Tesla: Tesla’s Gigafactory is a prime example of automation’s impact on automotive manufacturing. The factory uses over a thousand robots for tasks ranging from assembly to packaging. This extensive use of robotics has enabled Tesla to scale production rapidly while maintaining high standards of quality and efficiency.
  2. Toyota: Toyota has implemented advanced automation in its production processes, focusing on the integration of robots and human workers. Known as “Collaborative Robots” or “Cobots,” these machines work alongside human operators, enhancing productivity while ensuring safety and flexibility.

Challenges and Future Prospects

Despite the numerous benefits, the integration of automation and robotics in automotive manufacturing presents challenges. High initial costs, technical complexities, and the need for continuous maintenance and updates are significant considerations. Additionally, there is a need for skilled personnel to manage and maintain these advanced systems.

Looking forward, the future of automotive manufacturing will likely see increased collaboration between humans and robots. Emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning, and the Internet of Things (IoT) will further enhance the capabilities of automated systems. Predictive maintenance, real-time data analytics, and adaptive manufacturing processes are some areas where these technologies will have a profound impact.

Conclusion

The pivotal role of automation and robotics in transforming the automotive industry cannot be overstated. These technologies drive efficiency, precision, and innovation, positioning the industry for a future of sustained growth and competitiveness. As manufacturers continue to embrace and integrate advanced automation solutions, the potential for enhanced production capabilities and superior product quality will only expand.

By leveraging the full potential of automation and robotics, the automotive industry is set to achieve new heights of operational excellence and innovation, ultimately delivering greater value to consumers and stakeholders alike.

In the rapidly evolving automotive industry, the concept of lightweight design has emerged as a pivotal strategy for enhancing vehicle performance, efficiency, and sustainability. By reducing the weight of vehicles, manufacturers can achieve significant improvements in fuel economy, reduce emissions, and enhance overall driving dynamics. This article delves into the various aspects of lightweight design, exploring its benefits, materials, and technologies, and how it offers greater flexibility in automotive manufacturing.

The Importance of Lightweight Design

Lightweight design is essential for meeting stringent environmental regulations and consumer demands for more fuel-efficient vehicles. According to a report by the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), reducing vehicle weight by 10% can result in a 6-8% improvement in fuel economy. This is particularly crucial as the automotive industry shifts towards more sustainable practices to combat climate change and reduce carbon footprints.

Moreover, lightweight vehicles exhibit better performance characteristics, including improved acceleration, braking, and handling. This is because reducing the mass of the vehicle decreases the inertia, allowing for more responsive control. Additionally, lighter vehicles place less strain on tires, brakes, and suspension systems, leading to reduced wear and tear and lower maintenance costs.

Advanced Materials for Lightweight Design

The quest for lightweight vehicles has led to the development and adoption of advanced materials that offer high strength-to-weight ratios. These materials include high-strength steel, aluminum alloys, magnesium alloys, and composites.

  1. High-Strength Steel: While traditional steel is known for its durability, high-strength steel (HSS) offers comparable strength with significantly reduced weight. HSS is widely used in automotive structures and safety components due to its excellent energy absorption and crashworthiness.
  2. Aluminum Alloys: Aluminum is about one-third the weight of steel, making it an ideal material for lightweighting. Aluminum alloys are used extensively in body panels, engine blocks, and suspension components. They provide good corrosion resistance and recyclability, contributing to the overall sustainability of the vehicle.
  3. Magnesium Alloys: Magnesium is the lightest structural metal available, with a density about one-fourth that of steel. Magnesium alloys are used in various automotive components, including engine blocks, transmission cases, and steering wheels. Their use is particularly advantageous in applications where weight reduction is critical without compromising strength.
  4. Composites: Composite materials, such as carbon fiber reinforced polymers (CFRP) and glass fiber reinforced polymers (GFRP), offer exceptional strength-to-weight ratios. CFRP, in particular, is used in high-performance and luxury vehicles due to its lightweight and high-strength properties. Although expensive, the use of composites is growing as manufacturing techniques improve and costs decrease.

Technological Innovations in Lightweighting

Advancements in manufacturing technologies have played a crucial role in the adoption of lightweight materials. These technologies enable the efficient production and integration of lightweight components into vehicle structures.

  1. Additive Manufacturing: Also known as 3D printing, additive manufacturing allows for the creation of complex geometries that are difficult to achieve with traditional manufacturing methods. This technology is particularly useful for producing lightweight components with optimized designs that reduce material usage while maintaining strength and functionality.
  2. Multi-Material Joining: The integration of different materials in a single component can be challenging due to differences in their properties. However, advancements in joining technologies, such as friction stir welding, adhesive bonding, and laser welding, have made it possible to combine materials like aluminum and steel or composites and metals. These techniques ensure strong, durable joints without adding significant weight.
  3. Hot Stamping: This process involves heating steel blanks to a high temperature and then forming them into desired shapes using a stamping press. Hot stamping increases the strength of the material, allowing for the use of thinner and lighter steel sheets in vehicle structures. This technology is widely used in the production of safety-critical components, such as B-pillars and door beams.

Benefits of Lightweight Design in Automotive Manufacturing

The implementation of lightweight design principles offers several benefits that extend beyond fuel efficiency and performance improvements. These benefits include enhanced design flexibility, cost savings, and increased market competitiveness.

  1. Enhanced Design Flexibility: Lightweight materials and advanced manufacturing techniques allow designers to create more complex and innovative vehicle designs. For example, the use of aluminum and composites enables the production of intricate shapes and structures that are not possible with traditional materials. This flexibility allows manufacturers to optimize vehicle aerodynamics, improve interior space, and incorporate advanced safety features without adding weight.
  2. Cost Savings: While the initial cost of lightweight materials may be higher than traditional materials, the long-term savings can be substantial. Lighter vehicles require less energy to produce and operate, leading to reduced fuel consumption and lower operating costs. Additionally, the use of lightweight materials can reduce the overall number of components needed, simplifying assembly processes and reducing manufacturing costs.
  3. Increased Market Competitiveness: As consumer preferences shift towards more fuel-efficient and environmentally friendly vehicles, manufacturers that adopt lightweight design principles gain a competitive edge. Vehicles with improved fuel economy and reduced emissions are more attractive to consumers and comply with stringent regulatory requirements. This not only enhances brand reputation but also opens up new market opportunities.

Future Trends in Lightweight Design

The future of lightweight design in automotive manufacturing looks promising, with ongoing research and development aimed at discovering new materials and refining existing technologies. Some emerging trends include:

  1. Advanced Composites: Research is focused on developing next-generation composites that offer improved properties at lower costs. Innovations in nanotechnology and bio-based materials are expected to play a significant role in the future of lightweight design.
  2. Hybrid Materials: The combination of different materials at the molecular level, known as hybrid materials, is an area of active exploration. These materials aim to harness the best properties of each constituent, providing superior strength, durability, and weight savings.
  3. Smart Manufacturing: The integration of digital technologies, such as artificial intelligence (AI) and the Internet of Things (IoT), into manufacturing processes will enable more efficient production of lightweight components. Smart manufacturing techniques can optimize material usage, reduce waste, and improve quality control.

Final Takeaway

Lightweight design is a transformative approach in automotive manufacturing that offers numerous benefits, including improved fuel efficiency, enhanced performance, and greater design flexibility. The adoption of advanced materials and manufacturing technologies is driving the industry towards more sustainable and innovative solutions. As research and development continue to advance, the future of lightweight design holds even greater potential for revolutionizing the automotive sector.

By leveraging the advantages of lightweight materials and embracing cutting-edge technologies, automakers can meet the demands of modern consumers and regulatory standards, ensuring a competitive edge in the dynamic automotive market. The journey towards a lighter, more efficient, and environmentally friendly automotive industry is well underway, promising a brighter future for both manufacturers and consumers.

Just 8% of automotive OEMs see new tech-native EV entrants as threat, despite growing evidence that failure to invest in industrial digitalisation approaches may cost them the market

New research conducted by Wards Intelligence for Hexagon’s Manufacturing Intelligence division reveals that traditional carmakers are not prioritising the adoption of ‘industry 4.0’ processes, and risk being blindsided by new market entrants.

Big Tech and unicorn start-ups from Silicon Valley, the UK and China are disrupting the electric vehicles (EV) market with advanced digitalisation and automation capabilities, enabling time-to-market reductions from several years to as little as three months. The EV market is projected to soar to global sales of 34.7 million by 2030, and it is critical for OEMs to match the disruptors’ pace of innovation in order to participate. Despite this, only 48% of the auto industry report they plan to invest more in the types of Smart Manufacturing strategies their competitors are using, while 25% have no plans.

The new players benefit from the fact that low EV order volumes are helping speed overtake scale as the key factor in automotive manufacturing. This leaves conventional carmakers exposed to electronics companies such as Foxconn and Huawei, which are used to rapid innovation cycles and face low barriers because consumer’s expect more integrated digital technologies, new car designs render as many as 90% of automotive parts from existing ICE vehicles redundant, and their strategies that are less reliant on current supply chains and mass-manufacturing plants. The research finds that only 8% of carmakers see pure-play EV manufacturers as a threat, yet Tesla is already the market leader for battery electric vehicles (BEVs), while the likes of Lucid rapidly are scaling up with vertically-integrated EV production.

In contrast, the data reveals a disconnect in the equivalent progress towards digital transformation by larger OEMs. While there has been a definite uptick in interest around “smart” automotive manufacturing, the research shows that recognition of its value is not always accompanied with implementation. Instead, the data reveals that the industry sees sourcing the required volume of parts and inability to achieve economies of scale as the greatest perceived risk to the success of EV supply chains.

Paolo Guglielmini, President of Hexagon’s Manufacturing Intelligence division, said: “Our findings show that automakers’ challenges in getting a foothold in the new EV world order are as much internal as they are external. The need to make EVs more affordable at current order volumes, coupled with delivering greater choice or ‘cars-to-order’ means traditional high volume manufacturing lines must be replaced by more agile manufacturing, built around speed to market and possibly forgoing the ‘just-in-time’ model for good. The challenge for incumbents is doing so, while still achieving the required profit margins. This can only be resolved through vertical integration of the development and manufacturing processes, but this isn’t easy and necessitates a cultural shift to match the technology investments.

“Traditional automotive manufacturing business models are being reconfigured to prioritise flexibility over process, empowering people with more integrated processes and greater automation. Digital design and manufacturing approaches are key to agility and are a large part of the reason digital-natives are making waves in the market. The big OEMs are not standing still – we are seeing incredible innovation by companies that are having to overhaul their global operations at a rate of knots – but they need to embrace continuous improvement processes and revisit the degree and type of collaboration they have with their suppliers to ensure they come out on the winning side of the electric vehicle pivot.”

Overall, the data shows that there is growing interest in smart manufacturing approaches that apply pervasive connectivity, cloud and edge computing with momentum in data analytics, AI and robotics affirming that the industry is on the threshold of unprecedented change with very different ideas and levels of maturity on the journey to Industry 4.0. The convergence of these additional technologies will act as an adoption catalyst, with OEMs and their supply chains trying to adapt to this change as they evolve with the market.
